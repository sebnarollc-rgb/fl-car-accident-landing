What to do after a Florida car accident

Acting quickly protects your health and your claim. Here's what matters most.

  1. 1

    See a doctor within 14 days

    Florida's no-fault (PIP) law requires medical care within 14 days of the crash to keep your benefits. Don't wait, even if you feel okay.

  2. 2

    Document everything

    Photos of the scene, vehicle damage, and injuries. Get the police report number and the other driver's insurance info.

  3. 3

    Don't talk to their insurer

    The other driver's insurance company may call quickly. You're not required to give a statement — let an attorney handle it.

  4. 4

    Call an attorney before you settle

    Early settlement offers are often far below what your claim is worth. A free review tells you where you stand.

Common questions

How much does it cost to hire you?

Nothing upfront. We work on a contingency fee, which means we only get paid if we recover money for you. The case review is always free.

I already feel fine — do I still need a doctor?

Yes. Many injuries (like whiplash or concussions) show up days later. Florida law also requires medical care within 14 days to use your PIP benefits, so don't skip it.

The insurance company already called me. What do I say?

You're not required to give a recorded statement to the other driver's insurer. It's best to talk to an attorney first — call us before you say anything.

How long do I have to file a claim in Florida?

For most crashes on or after March 24, 2023, you generally have 2 years to file a lawsuit. Some situations are shorter. Don't wait to find out.

What if the accident was partly my fault?

You may still be able to recover. Florida uses a modified comparative negligence rule. A quick review can tell you where you stand.
